## Central thesis

ABM teams do not need a larger pile of prompts. They need a small operating system that separates:

1. **Context** — what the AI should know.
2. **Instructions** — what good work looks like.
3. **Tools** — what systems the AI may read or change.
4. **Workflows and agents** — how work moves from input to reviewed output.
5. **Governance** — where humans approve, measure, and improve the system.

## Audience promise

By the end, an ABM practitioner should be able to sketch a safe AI workspace for one real use case and know what belongs in a prompt, a reusable skill, an MCP connection, an agent, a repository, and a human approval step.

## Presenter guardrails

- Do not turn the talk into a tour of every AI product.
- Do not attempt a live MCP authorization flow.
- Use only synthetic company and contact information in the demonstration.
- Do not claim that agentic execution removes the need for marketing judgment.
- Do not position GitHub as the only possible repository; it is the demonstrated central-brain pattern.
- Do not publish or make the practical-skills repository public during the live session unless visibility and licensing were approved beforehand.
